## 1. Meaning and Definition

**Personality** is the sum total of the ways in which an individual reacts to and interacts
with others — the relatively stable pattern of thoughts, emotions and behaviours that
distinguishes one person from another.

**Gordon Allport's classic definition:**
> "The dynamic organisation within the individual of those psychophysical systems that
> determine his unique adjustments to his environment."

Unpacking that definition:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dynamic organisation** | Personality is not static; it is organised and continually developing |
| **Psychophysical systems** | Both mind (traits, attitudes, motives) and body (physique, glands, nervous system) |
| **Determine** | Personality causes behaviour — it is an active, not passive, force |
| **Unique adjustments** | Every individual adapts to the environment in a distinctive way |

**Key characteristics of personality:**
- It is **unique** to each individual
- It is **relatively stable and consistent** over time and across situations
- It is **integrated and organised** — a whole, not a collection of parts
- It is both **inherited and acquired**
- It is expressed through **behaviour**, and is therefore observable and measurable
- It involves **adjustment** to the environment

## 2. Determinants of Personality

### 2.1 Heredity (Nature)
Factors determined at conception — physical stature, facial attractiveness, gender,
temperament, muscle composition, reflexes, energy level, biological rhythms.

- Transmitted through **genes** located on chromosomes
- Evidence: **twin studies** — identical twins reared apart show remarkably similar
  personalities, suggesting genetics accounts for roughly **50%** of personality variation
- Sets the **outer limits**; environment determines development within those limits

### 2.2 Environment (Nurture)
- **Culture** — norms, values and beliefs of the society; establishes what behaviour is
  acceptable (e.g. Indian collectivist emphasis on family and hierarchy vs. Western
  individualism)
- **Family** — the earliest and most powerful influence; parenting style, birth order,
  socio-economic status, family size
- **Social/Group membership** — peer groups, friends, school, colleagues, reference groups
- **Life experiences** — successes, failures, traumas, role models

### 2.3 Situation
Personality traits are stable, but the **situation moderates their expression**. A person may
be deferential in a job interview and assertive with friends. Situations differ in
"**strength**":
- **Strong situations** (army, courtroom, exam hall) — clear rules; personality matters less
- **Weak situations** (unstructured team project) — ambiguous cues; personality shows through

> **Exam framing:** Personality = f (Heredity, Environment, Situation). Modern view is
> *interactionist* — nature and nurture jointly determine personality; the nature-vs-nurture
> debate is settled as "both."

## 3. Theories of Personality

### 3.1 Psychoanalytic Theory — Sigmund Freud

Personality has three structures in constant conflict:

| Structure | Principle | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Id** | Pleasure principle | Unconscious, instinctive drives; demands immediate gratification |
| **Ego** | Reality principle | Conscious mediator; satisfies the id within realistic constraints |
| **Superego** | Morality principle | Conscience; internalised social and parental standards |

- **Levels of consciousness:** conscious, preconscious, unconscious (iceberg analogy)
- **Defence mechanisms** used by the ego to manage anxiety: repression, projection,
  rationalisation, displacement, reaction formation, regression, sublimation
- **Psychosexual stages:** oral → anal → phallic → latency → genital
- **Criticism:** unscientific, unverifiable, over-emphasis on sex and childhood

### 3.2 Neo-Freudian / Psychosocial — Erik Erikson
Eight stages of development across the entire lifespan, each a crisis to resolve:
trust vs. mistrust → autonomy vs. shame → initiative vs. guilt → industry vs. inferiority →
identity vs. role confusion → intimacy vs. isolation → generativity vs. stagnation →
integrity vs. despair.

### 3.3 Trait Theories
Personality is a combination of measurable, enduring **traits**.

- **Allport:** *Cardinal* traits (dominate the whole personality), *Central* traits
  (5–10 general characteristics), *Secondary* traits (situational preferences)
- **Cattell's 16 PF:** distinguished **surface traits** (observable) from **source traits**
  (underlying causes); identified 16 primary source-trait factors
- **Eysenck:** three dimensions — Extraversion, Neuroticism, Psychoticism

### 3.4 Type Theories
- **Sheldon's somatotypes:** *Endomorph* (soft, round → sociable, relaxed);
  *Mesomorph* (muscular → assertive, energetic); *Ectomorph* (thin, fragile → restrained,
  introverted)
- **Hippocrates' humours:** sanguine, melancholic, choleric, phlegmatic
- **Jung's types:** introvert vs. extravert — the basis for the MBTI

### 3.5 Social Learning Theory — Albert Bandura
Personality is learned through **observation, imitation and modelling**, not just direct
reinforcement. Key concepts: *vicarious learning*, *self-efficacy*, and
**reciprocal determinism** — person, behaviour and environment influence each other mutually.

### 3.6 Self Theory — Carl Rogers
Centres on the **self-concept** (how one perceives oneself) and the drive toward
**self-actualisation**. Congruence between the *real self* and *ideal self* produces a
well-adjusted personality; incongruence produces anxiety. Growth requires
**unconditional positive regard**.

## 4. The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)

The most widely used personality instrument in the world (~2.5 million assessments a year).
Based on Jung's typology. Respondents are classified on four dichotomies:

| Dimension | Poles | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **E / I** | Extraverted vs. Introverted | Where do you draw energy from? |
| **S / N** | Sensing vs. Intuitive | How do you gather information? |
| **T / F** | Thinking vs. Feeling | How do you make decisions? |
| **J / P** | Judging vs. Perceiving | How do you orient to the outer world? |

These combine into **16 types** (e.g. INTJ, ESTJ, ENFP).

- **INTJ** — visionary, original, driven, sceptical
- **ESTJ** — organiser, practical, realistic, decisive; the classic administrator
- **ENTP** — conceptualiser, innovative, resourceful, quick

**Criticism (important for exams):**
- Forces people into either/or categories — no middle ground, though most people are moderate
- **Poor test-retest reliability** — many respondents get a different type when retested
- Weak evidence that MBTI predicts job performance
- Best used for **self-awareness, counselling and team-building**, *not* for selection

## 5. The Big Five Model (OCEAN / Five-Factor Model)

The most **research-supported and validated** framework in OB. Five broad dimensions:

| Factor | High scorer | Low scorer |
|---|---|---|
| **O** — Openness to Experience | Creative, curious, imaginative, artistically sensitive | Conventional, practical, prefers the familiar |
| **C** — Conscientiousness | Responsible, organised, dependable, persistent | Disorganised, unreliable, easily distracted |
| **E** — Extraversion | Gregarious, assertive, sociable, comfortable in relationships | Reserved, timid, quiet |
| **A** — Agreeableness | Cooperative, warm, trusting, good-natured | Cold, antagonistic, disagreeable |
| **N** — Neuroticism (inverse: Emotional Stability) | Anxious, insecure, depressed, prone to stress | Calm, self-confident, secure |

### Why it matters — links to work outcomes

- **Conscientiousness is the strongest and most consistent predictor of job performance
  across all occupations.** Conscientious employees develop more job knowledge, exert more
  effort and show higher persistence. (Downside: may be perfectionist, rigid, less adaptable
  to change.)
- **Emotional Stability** → highest life and job satisfaction, lowest stress levels
- **Extraversion** → strong performance in sales, management and other social occupations;
  emerges as leaders; higher social dominance. (Downside: impulsive, may be attention-seeking.)
- **Openness** → excels in training, adapts to organisational change, creative and
  innovative; associated with transformational leadership
- **Agreeableness** → better liked, higher compliance, more organisational citizenship
  behaviour, lower deviance. (Downside: lower salary/career success — too accommodating in
  negotiations.)

## 6. Other Personality Traits Relevant to OB

### 6.1 Core Self-Evaluation (CSE)
The bottom-line conclusion individuals hold about their own capability and worth. Composed of
**self-esteem, self-efficacy, locus of control and emotional stability**. People with positive
CSE set more ambitious goals, persist longer and report higher job satisfaction and performance.

### 6.2 Locus of Control (Julian Rotter)
- **Internals** — believe they control their own destiny; more proactive, higher job
  satisfaction, lower absenteeism, seek information, better suited to complex/autonomous jobs
- **Externals** — believe outcomes are governed by luck, fate or powerful others; more
  compliant and conforming, better suited to structured, routine, well-supervised work

### 6.3 Machiavellianism (Mach)
Named after Niccolò Machiavelli's *The Prince*. High Machs are pragmatic, maintain emotional
distance, manipulate more, win more and are persuaded less; they believe **"ends justify the
means."** They flourish when interacting face-to-face, in situations with minimal rules, and
where emotional involvement distracts others — e.g. negotiation, commission-based sales.

### 6.4 Self-Esteem
The degree to which individuals like or dislike themselves. High SEs take more risks, choose
unconventional jobs and are more satisfied; low SEs are more susceptible to external
influence and more dependent on approval.

### 6.5 Self-Monitoring
The ability to adjust behaviour to external situational factors.
- **High self-monitors** — chameleon-like, highly adaptable, receive better performance
  ratings, emerge as leaders, more mobile careers, more promotions
- **Low self-monitors** — display true dispositions consistently; high behavioural consistency

### 6.6 Risk-Taking Propensity
High risk-takers make faster decisions with less information. **Job–propensity fit matters:**
high propensity suits stock traders and entrepreneurs; low propensity suits auditors and
accountants.

### 6.7 Type A vs. Type B Personality (Friedman & Rosenman)

| Type A | Type B |
|---|---|
| Constant sense of time urgency | Never suffers from time urgency |
| Impatient; hurries the pace of events | Patient, relaxed, unhurried |
| Competitive, aggressive, hostile | Non-competitive, easy-going |
| Multitasks; struggles with leisure | Plays for fun, not to win |
| Measures success in numbers | Can relax without guilt |
| Fast decisions, quantity over quality | Better at deliberate, creative work |
| **Higher risk of coronary heart disease and stress** | Lower stress and health risk |

> Type As tend to do well in sales and jobs demanding speed; Type Bs do better in senior
> executive roles requiring judgement, patience and creativity.

### 6.8 Proactive Personality
Individuals who identify opportunities, show initiative, take action and persevere until
meaningful change occurs — rather than passively reacting. Associated with entrepreneurial
success, career advancement and being seen as leaders.

### 6.9 The Dark Triad
Socially undesirable traits that most people possess to some degree:
- **Machiavellianism** — manipulative, pragmatic, ends justify means
- **Narcissism** — grandiose sense of self-importance, requires admiration, sense of
  entitlement, arrogant; rated by bosses as *less* effective, especially in helping others
- **Psychopathy** — lack of concern for others, lack of guilt or remorse when actions cause harm

### 6.10 Other Traits
- **Authoritarianism** — belief in status and power differences in organisations
- **Dogmatism** — rigidity of beliefs; closed-mindedness
- **Achievement Orientation (nAch)** — drive to excel and succeed against a standard
- **Introversion / Extraversion** — orientation inward vs. toward the external world

## 7. Personality–Job Fit: Holland's Typology

John Holland's **Vocational Preference** theory argues that job satisfaction and turnover
depend on how well an individual's personality matches the occupational environment. Six types:

| Type | Personality characteristics | Congruent occupations |
|---|---|---|
| **Realistic** | Shy, stable, practical, conforming | Mechanic, farmer, assembly-line worker |
| **Investigative** | Analytical, curious, independent | Economist, mathematician, research analyst |
| **Artistic** | Imaginative, idealistic, emotional, unsystematic | Musician, writer, interior decorator |
| **Social** | Sociable, cooperative, understanding | Social worker, teacher, counsellor |
| **Enterprising** | Ambitious, energetic, self-confident, domineering | Lawyer, salesperson, manager |
| **Conventional** | Efficient, practical, conforming, unimaginative | Accountant, bank teller, corporate manager |

Represented in the **hexagonal model** — types adjacent on the hexagon are most similar;
diagonally opposite types are most dissimilar. **Key conclusion:** satisfaction is highest
and turnover lowest when personality and occupation are congruent.

## 8. Person–Organisation Fit

The individual should fit not just the job but the **organisation's culture**. A person's
personality must be compatible with the organisation's values.

- **Extraverts** → aggressive, team-oriented cultures
- **Highly agreeable** people → supportive, people-oriented cultures rather than aggressive ones
- **High openness** → innovative rather than standardised cultures

Good fit predicts higher **job satisfaction, organisational commitment and lower turnover**;
this is why organisations increasingly select for cultural fit, not just skills.

## 9. Measuring Personality

| Method | Description | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-report surveys** | Individual rates own traits (most common; e.g. Big Five inventories) | Faking/social desirability bias, especially in selection |
| **Observer-ratings** | Independent assessment by a colleague/supervisor | Rater bias; better predictor of job success than self-reports |
| **Projective tests** | Ambiguous stimuli interpreted by the subject — **Rorschach Inkblot Test**, **Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)** | Subjective scoring, low reliability |
| **Interviews & assessment centres** | Behavioural observation in structured exercises | Time-consuming and expensive |

## 10. Applications in Organisational Behaviour

Understanding personality helps managers to:
1. **Select** the right people — matching personality to job requirements (Big Five,
   especially conscientiousness, in selection)
2. **Place and transfer** employees into congruent roles (Holland, person–job fit)
3. **Design motivation systems** — internals respond to autonomy and goal-setting; externals
   to close supervision and clear direction
4. **Build effective teams** — balanced mix of traits; avoid all-similar teams
5. **Predict and manage stress** — identify Type A employees at risk; design interventions
6. **Develop leaders** — extraversion, conscientiousness and openness predict leader emergence
7. **Manage change** — high-openness employees adapt faster; use them as change agents
8. **Reduce conflict** — recognising trait differences (high Machs, low agreeableness)
   explains recurring interpersonal friction
9. **Anticipate deviance** — Dark Triad traits are linked to counterproductive work behaviour

## Quick Revision Summary

- **Personality** = dynamic organisation of psychophysical systems producing unique adjustment
  to the environment (Allport)
- **Determinants** = Heredity + Environment + Situation (interactionist view)
- **Theories** = Psychoanalytic (Freud), Psychosocial (Erikson), Trait (Allport, Cattell,
  Eysenck), Type (Sheldon, Jung), Social Learning (Bandura), Self (Rogers)
- **MBTI** = E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P → 16 types; popular but weak reliability
- **Big Five (OCEAN)** = most validated; **Conscientiousness is the best predictor of job
  performance**
- **Key OB traits** = Core self-evaluation, Locus of control, Machiavellianism, Self-esteem,
  Self-monitoring, Risk-taking, Type A/B, Proactive personality, Dark Triad
- **Holland's RIASEC** → personality–job fit drives satisfaction and reduces turnover
- **Person–organisation fit** → personality must match organisational culture
