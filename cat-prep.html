Section-wise Strategy

How I'm attacking each section

Section 1

VARC — Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

  • Read 30–45 minutes daily — editorials, essays, non-fiction — to build reading stamina.
  • RC is ~70% of the section: practice 2 passages a day with a timer.
  • For para jumbles & summaries, focus on logical flow, not grammar tricks.
  • Accuracy over attempts — eliminate two options, then decide.
Section 2

DILR — Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

  • Set selection is the game: spend the first 3–4 minutes scanning all sets and picking the easiest 3.
  • Practice full sets, not isolated questions — one set daily minimum.
  • Master common frameworks: arrangements, grouping, games & tournaments, Venn diagrams, tables/charts.
  • Know when to abandon a set — sunk-cost thinking kills this section.
Section 3

QA — Quantitative Ability

  • Arithmetic first (percentages, ratios, TSD, profit & loss) — it's the biggest chunk.
  • Then Algebra and Geometry; Number System and Modern Math last.
  • Maintain a formula + shortcuts notebook and revise it weekly.
  • Solve untimed for concepts first, then shift to timed sets for speed.

Study Plan

A phased roadmap to exam day

1
Phase 1 · Foundations · ~3 months

Build concepts from scratch

Cover the full QA syllabus topic by topic, start daily reading for VARC, and solve one DILR set a day. No mocks yet — the goal is coverage and clarity, not speed. Target: finish basics of every topic at least once.

2
Phase 2 · Practice & Speed · ~3 months

Timed sectional practice

Shift to timed sectional tests (2–3 per week), previous-year CAT papers, and topic-wise drills on weak areas. Take one full mock every two weeks to establish a baseline and get used to the interface.

3
Phase 3 · Mock Season · final 2–3 months

Mocks + deep analysis

1–2 full mocks per week, each followed by a 2–3 hour analysis session: why every wrong answer was wrong, which questions I should have skipped, and where time leaked. The analysis matters more than the mock itself.

4
Phase 4 · Taper · last 2 weeks

Revise, rest, peak on the day

No new topics. Revise the formula notebook and mock error log, take 2–3 light mocks at the actual slot time, fix the sleep schedule, and go in fresh.

Golden Rules

Things toppers repeat every year

🎯
Accuracy beats attempts.

A 99+ percentile rarely needs more than 60–65% of the paper attempted — attempted correctly.

🔍
Analyse every mock like a post-mortem.

A mock without 2 hours of analysis is a wasted mock. Keep an error log and revisit it weekly.

⏭️
Learn to skip.

The best test-takers are the best skippers. Every minute stuck on a bad question is a minute stolen from an easy one.

📈
Consistency > intensity.

Three focused hours daily beats a 12-hour Sunday binge. Momentum compounds.

🧘
Percentile panic is optional.

Mock percentiles fluctuate wildly. Track your own score trend, not the leaderboard.
