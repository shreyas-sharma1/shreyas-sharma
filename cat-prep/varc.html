Strategies that actually move your percentile

Reading Comprehension — the 70% of VARC
  • Read the passage first, fully, once. Question-first reading fails in CAT because most questions are inference-based, not fact-lookup. Read for structure: What is the author's point? How does each paragraph serve it?
  • Map the passage in one line per paragraph. Mentally tag each para: claim / evidence / counterargument / concession / conclusion. CAT questions are about this skeleton, not the details.
  • The author's opinion is gold. Underline (mentally) every evaluative word — "unfortunately", "remarkably", "critics argue... but". Tone and main-idea questions are decided by these.
  • Eliminate, don't select. Wrong options are wrong for findable reasons: too extreme (always/never/only), out of scope, distorted (right words, wrong relationship), or half-true. The right answer often feels bland — correct answers are defensible, not exciting.
  • Choose passages, not questions. In the first minute, skim all 4 passage openings and pick your best 3. A familiar topic + clear writing beats a short but dense passage.
  • Time budget: ~10 min per RC set (4 min reading, 6 min questions). If a question takes >90 seconds, mark and move.
  • Daily habit: one editorial (The Hindu / Indian Express / Aeon / Economist) every day, summarised in 3 sentences from memory. This single habit is worth more than any tricks.
Para Jumbles (TITA — no negative marking, always attempt)
  • Find the opener: a sentence that introduces the topic without pronouns ("he", "this", "such") or connectives ("however", "therefore") pointing backwards.
  • Lock mandatory pairs: noun → pronoun, idea → example ("for instance"), claim → contrast ("but/however/yet"), cause → effect ("as a result", "thus").
  • Chronology and generality: general statements precede specific ones; earlier events precede later ones.
  • Verify by reading your final order aloud in your head — flow breaks are easier to hear than to see.
  • Since these are TITA, attempt all of them — a 25% blind hit-rate costs nothing.
Para Summary questions
  • The right summary covers the whole passage, not just its most vivid sentence.
  • Kill options that: add new information, pick one example as the main point, use extreme language absent in the passage, or reverse the author's stance.
  • Write your own one-line summary before reading the options — then match. This immunises you against cleverly-worded traps.
Odd One Out
  • First find the theme connecting any three sentences confidently — the odd one is defined by the majority chain, not by "feel".
  • The odd sentence usually shares keywords with the paragraph but breaks its logical chain (different time period, different subject, or a claim the paragraph never builds on).
  • Test: can the remaining four be ordered into a coherent paragraph? If not, you removed the wrong one.
Vocabulary strategy — how to actually retain words
  • Learn words in context (the usage sentence), never as word-meaning pairs. CAT tests words inside passages and options, not in isolation.
  • Batch of 10 words/day → self-quiz the previous 3 days' batches before starting a new one (spaced repetition).
  • Group by charge: praise words (laud, magnanimous, sanguine) vs attack words (castigate, denigrate, diatribe) vs neutral-tone words (candid, pragmatic, empirical). Tone questions become trivial.
  • Use each new word once in your own sentence — production beats recognition.
